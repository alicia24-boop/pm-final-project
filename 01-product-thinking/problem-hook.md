# Problem Hook & Value Proposition (Module 1)

## Responses
- **Chosen path, which scenario are you committing to? (StreamLine or RouteLogic, or your own):** StreamLine Spotlight
- **Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?:** Engagement continues to plateau, high-value users churn, and StreamLine becomes another undifferentiated content library competing on volume instead of quality.
- **Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?:** Users are leaving the app to search Google, Reddit, reviews, and curated lists because browsing 15,000 titles makes it difficult to quickly find something worth watching.
- **Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:** We must prevent engagement and retention from declining by helping overwhelmed users quickly discover high-quality content they actually want to watch.
- **Value proposition, based on the proposed initiative (Spotlight or Velocity), what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:** For viewers overwhelmed by choice, Spotlight will deliver curated, high-quality recommendations so they can find something worth watching faster, while giving StreamLine a differentiated reason to stay and engage.

## Cold-read your own hook
- **Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?:** Yes. Plateauing engagement and potential churn threaten long-term retention and make it harder for StreamLine to differentiate in a crowded streaming market.
- **Is the moment of misery a systemic problem or just an edge case?:** It appears systemic because choice overload affects the core experience of browsing a catalog with 15,000 titles, especially for users who value quality over volume.
- **Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle you identified, or is it a generic feature?:** Yes. Spotlight directly addresses choice overload by reducing the effort required to find relevant, high-quality content rather than simply adding more content or another browsing feature.
